How to read this script:
- **Bold lines** are said out loud. Short lines are easier to memorize, so each one is a single breath.
- *[Brackets]* are stage directions, not lines.
- `{braces}` are numbers to fill in after the dry run. If we didn't measure it, we don't say it.
- About 150 spoken words a minute. Each section lists its word count.

### Demo fallbacks

- **Puck didn't upload the intro in time** → the setup call opens with the normal question. Everything else is the same.
- **Puck offline all morning** → the Today card uses seeded data. Don't mention it unless asked.
- **The 1:1 call doesn't connect within 5 s** → skip to Beat 2 with a teammate who's already enrolled ("here's someone Mary already knows").
- **Group call isn't in recents** → FaceTime → New FaceTime → Mary + Calypso⁻¹ → Video. Eric does this, not the judge.
- **A card is slow** → point at the dashboard: "Here's the decision being made."
- **Wrong or uncertain match** → it should show nothing, or "Getting to know someone new…". Say: **"That's the safety rule working. It won't guess."**
- **The judge goes off script** → let the agent finish, then Eric says "Let's call Mary" and points at cue 2.

## Q&A prep (2:00)

Keep each answer to **20–30 seconds**. The owner answers; anyone can add one sentence. If you don't know, say what we'd measure and how.

**1. "What if it shows the wrong person?"** (Vishnu)
That's our number-one rule. A match needs a face score of at least 0.40 and a lead of at least 0.08 over the next person, and the voice can't point to someone else. Otherwise it's "uncertain" or "Getting to know someone new," never a guess. There's a test for exactly the two-similar-relatives case. Places work the same way: below 0.6 confidence we say "somewhere with café sounds" instead of inventing a name.

**2. "Privacy? It's recording a vulnerable person all day. And it recorded me."** (Matt + Vishnu)
It did, and we'll say that plainly: we asked you to introduce yourself while the puck was on. In a product, people who talk with Mary would be told she wears one. We store numbers for faces and voices, not the audio. Data goes to the family's own backend, and every API call needs a token. Photos that look like IDs or cards are flagged and never shown. Consent is a real issue, and we don't claim to have solved it in 24 hours.

**3. "Why FaceTime instead of your own app?"** (Keanu)
Because Mary already knows how to answer FaceTime, and a new app is the hardest thing to ask of someone with dementia. Chert puts our agent into a real FaceTime call. The cards are Calypso⁻¹'s own video, so nothing gets installed on Mary's side.

**4. "How fast is it?"** (Keanu)
Our target is 2.5 seconds or less from the end of a sentence to the card on screen, and the dashboard times every step. In our dry runs we hit `{measured}`. If a recall answer takes over 4 seconds, the agent drops it instead of showing a stale card.

**5. "How does it learn people with no data on day one?"** (Keanu)
You just saw it. Each relative does one short FaceTime with Calypso⁻¹. Claude pulls out their name and how they know Mary, and we save their face and voice. Voices the puck heard earlier get matched once the person is known. That's how it knew you before you called.

**6. "Group calls: how do you tell who's who?"** (Keanu)
`{AGENT_TRACK_MODE}`. If Chert gives us a video track per person, each face maps to its person. If it's one combined grid, we find every face in the frame and identify each one. Both sit behind one setting.

**7. "What happens with bad Wi-Fi?"** (Matt)
The puck never depends on the network. Every clip goes to disk first. When it reconnects it uploads oldest first, and the server ignores duplicates, so hours offline cost nothing.

**8. "Is this a medical device? Does it slow dementia?"** (Eric)
No, and we're careful not to claim that. It's a communication aid that helps families have a better call. No reminders, no medication, no diagnosis.

**9. "Why Claude and Grok?"** (Vishnu)
Claude Haiku for fast, cheap decisions, like "is this question about Mary's day." Claude Sonnet for the writing, like daily summaries and setup calls. Grok for speech-to-text and text-to-speech. Faces and voices use open-source models we host ourselves. Each provider can be swapped with one setting.

**10. "What's next?"** (Eric)
A pilot with memory-care families, tuning the thresholds on real faces and lighting, and a view for caregivers. We're keeping medical claims out until there's evidence.

**11. "Why the name?"** (Eric)
In the Odyssey, Calypso kept Odysseus on her island and made him forget home. Calypso⁻¹ is the inverse: it brings people back home to the people they love.
